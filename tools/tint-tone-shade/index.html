Tint vs. Tone vs. Shade: The Birren Color Triangle

In classical color theory (Faber Birren and Wilhelm Ostwald's color triangle), every chromatic variation of a pure hue is formed by combining three elements—Pure Hue, White, and Black:

Color Term Mixing Formula Visual Effect in Painting & Design
Tint Pure Hue + White (#FFFFFF) Raises value (lightness) and softens saturation to create pastels, lit planes, and airy UI backgrounds.
Tone Pure Hue + Neutral Gray (White + Black) Reduces chroma (saturation) while preserving mid-tone value—essential for natural painting halftones and cohesive palettes.
Shade Pure Hue + Black (#000000) Lowers value (darkness) to create deep shadows, dark accents, and high-contrast borders.

Customizing Gray Value When Toning a Color

Most online tint and shade generators only mix a fixed 50% middle gray (#808080) for tones. However, painters frequently mix light grays or dark grays to match the exact value of their base pigment so the color desaturates without getting lighter or darker. Use the Mixer Neutral slider in the bottom section to set any gray value between 0% Black and 100% White.
