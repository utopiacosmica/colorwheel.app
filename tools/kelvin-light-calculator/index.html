Understanding the Purkinje Effect for Nocturne Painters & Lighting Artists

In 1825, Czech physiologist Jan Evangelista Purkyně noticed while walking through a garden at dusk that red geraniums—brighter than blue-green leaves at noon—turned pitch-dark before twilight ended, while the leaves and blue blossoms continued to gleam. Because human rod cells peak at 507nm (blue-green) and are nearly blind beyond 640nm (red), realistic night scenes in oil painting, illustration, and 3D lighting must darken warm reds dramatically while preserving cool cyan-slate values.
