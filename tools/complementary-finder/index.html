RYB vs. RGB Complementary Colors: Why Painters & Screens Differ

Most online calculators simply add 180° to the RGB hue angle, which pairs Red with Cyan and Blue with Yellow. While that works for additive light on screens, traditional painters and color theorists work on the RYB (Red–Yellow–Blue) wheel where primary pigments oppose secondary mixtures:

How to Use the Mute / Neutralize Slider for Shadows & Earth Tones

In oil, acrylic, watercolor, and digital painting, darkening a color with pure black deadens its saturation. Instead, artists add 10% to 50% of the complementary color to knock down over-saturated hues into atmospheric mid-tones and natural shadows. Drag the Mute / Neutralize Mix slider in the bottom half of the tool to preview and copy the exact muted HEX code.
