Free Simultaneous Contrast & Color Relativity Compensator

Every color in painting and design is seen in relation to its surroundings. Our free Simultaneous Contrast & Color Relativity Compensator helps painters, illustrators, and UI designers predict how a dominant background color distorts an adjacent subject color—and calculates the exact counter-adjusted HEX code required to keep your color looking true.

How Simultaneous Contrast Distorts Color (Chevreul & Josef Albers)

When you stare at a strong background color, the photoreceptors in your retina fatigue and project a complementary opponent afterimage onto neighboring shapes. This creates three predictable optical shifts:

Background Property Optical Illusion on Subject Color How to Counter-Compensate
Dark Background Subject appears lighter and chalkier than it truly is Lower the subject's brightness slightly toward the dark background
Light Background Subject appears darker and heavier than it truly is Raise the subject's brightness slightly toward the light background
Warm Red / Orange BG Subject shifts cooler toward Cyan / Blue-Green Warm the subject slightly toward Red-Orange to neutralize the cool push
Cool Blue / Green BG Subject shifts warmer toward Orange / Magenta-Red Cool the subject slightly toward the background hue
High-Chroma Vivid BG Muted subject colors look grayed-out and lifeless Boost subject saturation by 10%–20% to hold its chroma

Using Compensate Mode vs. Illusion Mode

  • COMPENSATE MODE (Default): Calculates the counter-shifted pigment or HEX code you should actually apply so that when placed against the top background color, the human eye perceives your intended target color.
  • ILLUSION MODE: Simulates the optical afterimage shift itself, showing how an unadjusted subject color will falsely appear to the viewer when surrounded by the top background color.
