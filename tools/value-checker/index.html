Why Standard HSB & HSL Sliders Mislead Painters and Designers

In digital color pickers, pure Yellow (#FFFF00) and pure Blue (#0000FF) both display 100% Brightness in HSB and 50% Lightness in HSL. However, human vision is far more sensitive to yellow-green wavelengths than blue. When converted to perceptual grayscale (CIE Relative Luminance and Lightness L*), Yellow reads as a high-key light (Value 9.3 / 10) while Blue reads as a deep shadow (Value 3.0 / 10).

Artist 0–10 Tonal Value Scale Reference

Value Scale (0–10) Perceptual Lightness (L*) Tonal Zone in Painting & Design
9.0 – 10.0 90% – 100% Specular Highlights & High-Key Whites
7.0 – 8.9 70% – 89% Light Mass & Direct Lit Planes
4.0 – 6.9 40% – 69% Mid-Tones & Halftones (Core Local Color)
1.5 – 3.9 15% – 39% Form Shadows & Cast Shadows
0.0 – 1.4 0% – 14% Deep Accents & Occlusion Darks

How to Find Different Colors with the Same Value (Isoluminant Colors)

By default, the bottom section displays the pure Grayscale Value of your top color. When you drag the Equal-Value Hue or Equal-Value Saturation sliders in the bottom section, the tool automatically calculates the exact brightness needed to keep the perceptual value locked to your top color. Click GRAYSCALE at any time to snap back to 0% saturation.
