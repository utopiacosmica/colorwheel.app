Why RYB, OKLCH, and RGB Give Different Harmony Colors

On a standard digital RGB wheel, pure Blue (240°) sits opposite Yellow (60°), whereas on Johannes Itten’s traditional RYB Painter Wheel, Blue sits opposite Orange and Red sits opposite Green. Meanwhile, OKLCH rotates hues inside a perceptually uniform space so a Triadic yellow, magenta, and cyan share consistent perceived lightness instead of jumping wildly in visual weight.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between Split-Complementary and Triadic color schemes?

A Triadic scheme spaces three colors equally at 120° intervals around the wheel, creating bold energy across all three primaries. A Split-Complementary scheme takes your base color and flanks its opposite at 180° ± 30°, preserving strong warm/cool contrast while keeping two of the colors closely related.

Should I use the RYB, OKLCH, or RGB color wheel?

Choose RYB Painter Wheel for fine art, oil/acrylic painting, and classic illustration color scripts. Choose OKLCH Perceptual for accessible web apps, UI kits, and design tokens. Choose RGB Digital when matching standard CSS hsl() hue angles.

How do you apply the 60-30-10 color rule to a harmony?

Dedicate 60% of your canvas or screen area to a calm dominant color, 30% to a supporting secondary hue, and 10% to your most saturated accent color. Check the live 60–30–10 Visual Balance Preview bar above to test how your palette reads in realistic proportions.
