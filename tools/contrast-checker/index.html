Understanding WCAG Text Sizes & Non-Text Contrast

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) set minimum luminance ratios so people with low vision, aging eyesight, color vision deficiency, or sunlit mobile screens can read content effortlessly:

  • Normal Body Text (WCAG 1.4.3 — Level AA): Text smaller than 24px (18pt) regular or smaller than 18.66px (14pt) bold must reach at least 4.5:1 (or 7.0:1 for Level AAA under WCAG 1.4.6).
  • Large Text: Headlines at 24px+ regular weight or 18.66px+ bold weight require at least 3.0:1 for Level AA and 4.5:1 for Level AAA.
  • UI Components & Graphics (WCAG 1.4.11): Interactive form borders, toggle switches, focus rings, and meaningful icons must maintain at least 3.0:1 against adjacent surfaces.

How WCAG Relative Luminance Contrast Is Calculated

Unlike simple RGB brightness averages, WCAG linearizes each sRGB channel and weights them by human photopic eye sensitivity (L = 0.2126R + 0.7152G + 0.0722B), reflecting the fact that green light appears far brighter than pure blue. The contrast ratio is then computed as (L1 + 0.05) / (L2 + 0.05), where L1 is the lighter color and L2 is the darker color—spanning from 1.00:1 (identical colors) to 21.00:1 (pure black #000000 against pure white #FFFFFF).

Frequently Asked Questions

What contrast ratio is required for WCAG AA and WCAG AAA?

WCAG AA requires 4.5:1 for normal text and 3.0:1 for large text and UI elements. WCAG AAA raises the requirement to 7.0:1 for normal text and 4.5:1 for large text.

What counts as large text in WCAG accessibility guidelines?

WCAG defines large text as 18pt (24px) or larger at normal weight, or 14pt (18.66px) or larger when bold (font-weight: 700+).

Does swapping foreground and background change the WCAG contrast ratio?

Mathematically the WCAG luminance ratio stays identical in both directions, which is why the top panel previews Color 2 text on Color 1 and the bottom panel previews Color 1 text on Color 2 so you can judge optical halation as well.
