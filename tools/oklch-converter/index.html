Why Designers and Painters Are Switching from HSL/HSB to OKLCH

Traditional HSL and HSB color pickers distort human lightness perception: pure Yellow (#FFFF00) and pure Blue (#0000FF) both claim to have 50% Lightness in HSL, even though Yellow is nearly as bright as white and Blue is dark. OKLCH solves this by modeling how human eyes actually perceive lightness and chroma:

OKLCH Channel Value Range How It Works in Design & Painting
L (Perceptual Lightness) 0% to 100% (or 0.0 to 1.0) Controls true tonal value. Any two colors with L = 68% have the exact same perceived lightness and contrast ratio.
C (Chroma) 0.00 to ~0.37 Controls perceived saturation intensity from neutral gray (0) to maximum pigment/screen vividness.
H (Perceptual Hue) 0° to 360° Rotates around the perceptual color wheel (~29° Red, ~90° Yellow, ~145° Green, ~260° Blue, ~320° Purple).

Automatic sRGB Gamut Mapping for HEX Export

Because OKLCH encompasses wide-gamut Display P3 and Rec.2020 colors, certain high-chroma combinations exceed standard sRGB HEX limits. When you push Chroma beyond the sRGB boundary for a given Hue and Lightness, our converter automatically finds the closest in-gamut sRGB Chroma while keeping your exact Lightness (L) and Hue (H) intact.
